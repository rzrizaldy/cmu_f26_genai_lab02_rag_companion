How does a machine read your resume?

Retrieval-augmented generation is a stack of five layers: cut documents into pieces, turn the pieces into numbers, find the nearest pieces and let an LLM answer from them, check that it worked, then put it to use. Click a layer to pull it out.

    3 · Find the nearest pieces, then answer

    Your question becomes a vector too. We score every chunk with cosine similarity and keep the top k. Only those chunks go to the LLM, which is told to answer from them and cite [n].

    4 · Check that it worked

    48 test questions, each written from one specific posting. A strategy scores when that posting comes back. Hit@5 = share found in the top 5. MRR = average of 1/rank (1.0 means always first). Bars are 95% bootstrap ranges: when they overlap, you can't call a winner.

    This is the idea behind Google's auto-rag-eval: let an LLM write questions from your own documents, have critics filter them, then score retrieval automatically. Here a TA wrote the 48, and the notebook shows the LLM version live.

    Read this as a mirror, not a verdict. The score is meaning-similarity between your words and a posting's words. It can't see formatting, references, or the person; postings carry their own biases; and real screening tools (and hiring law, like NYC Local Law 144 and the EU AI Act's high-risk rules for hiring) involve much more. Use the gaps to decide what to say more clearly. Don't use them to stuff keywords.
