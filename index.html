How does a machine read your resume?

This page shows how a RAG system works, one layer at a time. First we cut documents into pieces, then turn each piece into numbers, then find the pieces closest to a question and let an LLM answer from them. After that we check if it worked, and at the end you can try it with your own resume. Click a layer on the right, or use the buttons below.

1 · Cut it up

Before a model can search documents, we cut them into smaller pieces called chunks. The way we cut changes what the search can find later.

2 · Turn pieces into numbers

EmbeddingGemma reads each chunk and gives back a list of 256 numbers (an "embedding"). Chunks with similar meaning get similar numbers. On this map, each dot is one chunk from the 424 job postings.

How to use this step
  1. Type a skill or a short phrase and press Go. An amber dot shows where your phrase lands, next to the chunks with the closest meaning.
  2. Hover over any dot to read that chunk.
  3. Use "Are these two close?" to compare two phrases with a score from 0 to 1.

    3 · Find the closest pieces, then answer

    Your question also becomes 256 numbers. We compare it with every chunk and keep the top k most similar ones. Only those chunks are given to the LLM (Gemma), and it is told to answer only from them and to cite them as [1], [2].

    How to use this step
    1. Type a question, or click one of the examples.
    2. Press Search. You see the top chunks and their similarity scores.
    3. Open "What Gemma would receive" to see the exact prompt. This page does not call Gemma itself; the notebook does.

    4 · Check if it works

    We wrote 48 test questions. Each one comes from one specific posting, so we know the right answer. A strategy does well when it brings that posting back near the top.

    How to read this step
    1. Hit@5 is the share of questions where the right posting is in the top 5 results.
    2. MRR is the average of 1 ÷ rank. If the right posting is always first, MRR = 1.0; if it is usually second, MRR is around 0.5.
    3. The light bar around each dot is a 95% range. When two bars overlap a lot, the difference between them may just be luck, because 48 questions is a small test.
    4. Click any question in the table to see where each strategy ranked it.

    This idea comes from Google's auto-rag-eval: an LLM writes test questions from your own documents, other LLMs act as critics and filter them, and then the questions are used to score the search automatically. In the notebook you can watch Gemma write a few questions live.

    How to read this score. The score shows how close the meaning of your resume text is to the posting text. It cannot see your formatting, your references or how you do in an interview, and the postings themselves can be biased. Real hiring tools are more complicated, and in some places they are regulated (for example NYC Local Law 144 and the EU AI Act). Use the "weakest links" to see what you could explain more clearly. Please don't just copy keywords into your resume.
